In September 2024, [Zillow added climate data from First Street to every US listing to provide visibility into flood, fire, wind, heat, and air quality risk.](https://investors.zillowgroup.com/investors/news-and-events/news/news-details/2024/Zillow-introduces-First-Streets-comprehensive-climate-risk-data-on-for-sale-listings-across-the-US/default.aspx)

In December 2025, [Zillow removed the climate risk data from its listings due to pressure from the real estate industry, which has claimed that opaque and inaccurate scores have unfairly depressed sales prices.](https://www.theguardian.com/environment/2025/dec/01/zillow-removes-climate-risk-data-home-listings)

While a link out to the First Street website still exists on each listing, the information is made less accessible, and users have to actively seek it out.

Sweeping these risks under the carpet does not make them go away. Even if the real estate market is not evaluating these risks, the insurance industry certainly is.
Prospective homebuyers deserve to be fully informed in their buying decision.
